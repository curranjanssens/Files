# FAAH Inhibitor + Exogenous Anandamide: Research Summary

## Research Question
Has anyone tried or suggested combining FAAH (Fatty Acid Amide Hydrolase) inhibitors with exogenous anandamide administration in humans?

---

## Key Finding: No Direct Human Trials Found

**After extensive searching, no published human clinical trials were found that specifically combined FAAH inhibitor drugs with exogenously administered anandamide.** The scientific and clinical literature focuses almost exclusively on two separate approaches:

1. **FAAH inhibitors alone** (to elevate endogenous anandamide)
2. **Exogenous anandamide analogs** (without FAAH inhibition)

---

## Why This Combination Hasn't Been Tested

### 1. Pharmacological Rationale
FAAH inhibitors work by preventing the breakdown of **endogenous** anandamide. The therapeutic hypothesis is that elevating the body's own anandamide at physiologically relevant synapses is preferable to flooding all cannabinoid receptors with exogenous compounds.

### 2. Bioavailability Challenges
- Anandamide has extremely poor oral bioavailability
- It is rapidly degraded by FAAH within minutes of administration
- Most in vivo studies use IV or IP injection routes, not oral

### 3. Safety Concerns
- The BIA 10-2474 disaster (2016) killed one participant and injured five others in a FAAH inhibitor trial
- Regulatory caution around endocannabinoid-modulating drugs remains high

---

## Evidence from Animal Studies

### FAAH Knockout Mice + Exogenous Anandamide
Mice lacking FAAH (FAAH−/−) display **supersensitivity to exogenous anandamide**:

> "FAAH knockout mice are severely impaired in their ability to degrade anandamide and when treated with this compound, exhibit an array of intense CB1-dependent behavioral responses, including hypomotility, analgesia, catalepsy, and hypothermia."

Source: [PNAS - Supersensitivity to anandamide](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.161191698)

### URB597 + Anandamide in Primates
The FAAH inhibitor URB597 **markedly increased the potency (32-fold) and duration of action of anandamide** in rhesus monkeys.

> "URB597 does not mimic the effects of CB1 receptor agonists because its action is limited to potentiating anandamide action only at synapses at which it is tonically active."

Source: [PMC - FAAH inhibitor URB597 interactions with anandamide in rhesus monkeys](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3188916/)

### Conditioned Place Preference Studies
In rats pre-treated with URB597, anandamide produced dose-related conditioned place aversions (not seen without FAAH inhibition).

## Human Clinical Trials with FAAH Inhibitors Alone

### JNJ-42165279 for PTSD (2025)
- N = 100 patients with PTSD
- 12-week double-blind, placebo-controlled study
- **Result**: FAAH inhibition successfully elevated anandamide levels but showed **no effect on PTSD symptoms**

Source: [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40382500/)

### JNJ-42165279 for Social Anxiety Disorder
- Multicenter, double-blind, placebo-controlled study
- **Result**: Some evidence of anxiolytic effect, but trough concentrations appeared insufficient for complete FAAH inhibition

Source: [Nature - Neuropsychopharmacology](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41386-020-00888-1)

### PF-04457845 for Osteoarthritic Pain (2011)
- Phase II clinical trial
- **Result**: Did not meet efficacy endpoints

Source: [PMC - Systems Pharmacology Perspective](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3910012/)

### V158866 Pharmacokinetic Study
- Healthy volunteers
- **Result**: Dose-related increases in plasma anandamide confirmed

Source: [PMC - V158866 Safety Study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4875922/)

## The Jo Cameron Case: Nature's "FAAH + High Anandamide" Experiment

Jo Cameron, a Scottish woman, represents a natural human experiment in extreme FAAH dysfunction:

### Genetic Profile
- Heterozygous microdeletion in **FAAH-OUT** (a pseudogene regulating FAAH expression)
- **rs324420 C385A** mutation in FAAH gene

### Phenotype
- **70% higher circulating anandamide** than normal population
- **Triple normal PEA levels**
- Virtually no pain sensitivity (didn't notice severe osteoarthritis requiring surgery)
- Scores **0/21 on anxiety disorder questionnaire**
- Unable to experience fear
- Accelerated wound healing
- Some short-term memory impairment

> "She was immune to anxiety, unable to experience fear, and insensitive to pain."

Sources:
- [PMC - Microdeletion in FAAH pseudogene](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6676009/)
- [Wikipedia - Jo Cameron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jo_Cameron)
- [Smithsonian Magazine](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/smart-news/scottish-woman-doesnt-feel-pain-now-researchers-think-they-know-why-180971822/)

## CBD as a Natural FAAH Modulator + Anandamide

### Schizophrenia Trial
- Double-blind CBD vs amisulpride comparison
- CBD treatment accompanied by **significant increase in serum anandamide**
- Anandamide increase correlated with clinical improvement

Source: [Nature - Translational Psychiatry](https://www.nature.com/articles/tp201215)

### Cannabis Use Disorder Trial
- 800 mg CBD/day prevented the decline in anandamide seen with placebo
- CBD inhibits FAAH with EC50 of ~8.6 μM

Source: [Nature - Translational Psychiatry](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41398-023-02410-9)

## Supplement/Biohacking Approaches Discussed Online

While no formal trials exist, some sources discuss theoretical "stacks":

### Natural FAAH Inhibitors
| Compound | IC50/Ki | Source |
|----------|---------|--------|
| Kaempferol | Ki = 5 μM | Broccoli, kale, apples |
| CBD | EC50 ≈ 8.6 μM | Cannabis |
| Ginger (Zingiber) | 6x more active than kaempferol | Ginger root |
| Maca (Macamides) | Not quantified | Maca root |
| Dark chocolate | Contains OEA & LEA | Cacao |

### Exogenous Anandamide Sources
- Raw cacao contains trace anandamide
- PEA (palmitoylethanolamide) works synergistically with anandamide
- Commercial "anandamide supplements" are often just cacao powder

**Caveat**: Scientists believe dietary intake of kaempferol is unlikely to produce meaningful FAAH inhibition:
> "It is unlikely that this could be upscaled sufficiently to make FAAH inhibition occur through dietary intake."

Sources:
- [PMC - Kaempferol FAAH inhibition](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2538700/)
- [AEAJoy - FAAH Inhibitor Supplements](https://aeajoy.com/faah-inhibitor-supplements/)

## Palmitoylethanolamide (PEA): The Practical Alternative

PEA is an endogenous fatty acid amide that enhances anandamide activity (entourage effect):

### Human Trial Evidence
- 48 RCTs across neuropsychiatric, neurological, and pain conditions
- Doses: 300-1200 mg/day
- 2017 meta-analysis (786 patients): Significant pain reduction vs controls (P < 0.001)
- Formulations: Ultramicronized/micronized for better bioavailability

Sources:
- [PMC - PEA for Health Management](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8157570/)
- [ScienceDirect - PEA RCT Systematic Review](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2666354624002059)

## Summary Table: Approaches to Elevating Anandamide Signaling

| Approach | Human Trials? | Notes |
|----------|---------------|-------|
| FAAH inhibitor alone | Yes (multiple) | Mixed efficacy results; safety concerns |
| Exogenous anandamide alone | No (animals only) | Poor bioavailability; rapid degradation |
| **FAAH inhibitor + exogenous anandamide** | **NO** | Never tested in humans |
| CBD (indirect FAAH modulation) | Yes | Modest effects on plasma anandamide |
| PEA (entourage effect) | Yes (48 RCTs) | Enhances endogenous anandamide action |
| Natural FAAH inhibitors (diet) | No formal trials | Likely insufficient for clinical effect |

---

## Conclusions

1. **No human trials have combined FAAH inhibitors with exogenous anandamide**

2. Animal studies confirm the combination produces **dramatically potentiated effects** (32-fold increase in anandamide potency with URB597)

3. The Jo Cameron case demonstrates that chronically elevated anandamide (from genetic FAAH dysfunction) produces profound analgesia, anxiolysis, and accelerated healing

4. Clinical FAAH inhibitor trials (without exogenous anandamide) have shown **mixed results** and **safety concerns**

5. The practical approach most resembling this combination in humans involves:
   - CBD (moderate FAAH inhibition) + PEA (anandamide potentiation)
   - But this is far weaker than pharmacological FAAH inhibition + IV anandamide

6. Future research directions suggested by the Jo Cameron case focus on **FAAH-OUT targeting** rather than direct FAAH inhibition
