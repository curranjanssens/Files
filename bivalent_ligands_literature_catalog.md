# Catalog of Published Bivalent / Heterobivalent Ligands Targeting Receptor Dimers

Scope: ligands already synthesized and reported in the peer-reviewed literature whose explicit design goal is to bridge a receptor **dimer or heterodimer** (two pharmacophores joined by a spacer). Excludes in-house/unpublished compounds. "Optimal spacer" = the linker length the authors identified as best for true bivalent (two-protomer) engagement.

Compiled June 2026 from primary literature and field reviews. Terminology note: *bivalent* = two pharmacophores + spacer bridging two protomers; *bitopic/dualsteric* = one molecule engaging an orthosteric + an allosteric site on a **single** protomer (noted but flagged as a distinct class); *chimeric/fused* = pharmacophores merged with no tunable spacer (flagged).

## 8. Muscarinic acetylcholine (flagged — dualsteric/bitopic, NOT dimer-bridging)

The Mohr/Holzgrabe "bivalent" muscarinic ligands (iper-6/8-phth, iper-6/8-naph, **N-8-Iper**, atropine/scopolamine-6-phth) are **dualsteric/bitopic** — orthosteric iperoxo/atropine + allosteric (W84/naphthalimide) on a **single** M2/M1 protomer, hexa-/octa-methylene (6–8 C) linker tuning bias/partial agonism. No robust report of a true muscarinic receptor-**dimer**-bridging bivalent. (Antony 2009 *FASEB J*; Bock/Mohr 2014 *Nat Chem Biol*, *J Med Chem*.)

## 11. Other families / negatives

- **Histamine H2 (richest histamine bivalent chemistry):** Buschauer acylguanidine/hetarylpropylguanidine **H2 agonist** homodimers — Birnkammer 2012 (diacid, **C8–C10 optimal**, ~4000× histamine; a ~20-CH₂ "two-orthosteric-site" spacer was inactive → second pharmacophore is allosteric, not dimer-bridging); Pockes 2018 (**UR-Po448** C8, **UR-Po461**). *J Med Chem* 2012, PMID 22216892.
- **Histamine H1/H2 heterobivalent:** mepyramine (H1 antagonist) + cyanoguanidine (H2 antagonist); **6-methylene** optimal for H1. Sadek 2013, *Molecules* 18:14186.
- **Histamine H3:** no true homobivalent; only dual-target melatonin/H3 ligands (pentyl/hexyl, µM) — Pala/Spadoni, *IJMS* 2014, PMC4200786.
- **Muscarinic (dualsteric, §8):** iperoxo-W84/naphthalimide hybrids — bitopic, single protomer, NOT dimer-bridging.
- **GABA-B:** **genuinely empty** — no dimeric baclofen / antagonist / PAM, no bitopic agonist-PAM, despite obligate GB1/GB2 heterodimer. Mechanistic reason: single orthosteric site (GB1 VFT) + separate allosteric site (GB2 7TM). "GABA bivalents" in the literature (e.g., Xli-093) are GABA-**A**/benzodiazepine, a different receptor family.
- **Melanocortin MC4R:** homodimer bivalent **CJL-1-124** (20 atoms, cAMP EC50 ~4.7 nM) — from GPCR-dimer review.
- **Estrogen receptor:** homodimer bivalent **AK-15b** (6 atoms) — review-level.

## Cross-cutting design rules (verified across sources)

1. **Optimal spacer is target-specific**, ranging from ~5 to ~88 atoms — there is no universal length.
2. **Class A GPCR heterodimers cluster at ~18–22 atoms** (MDAN-21, KDN-21, MCC22, MMG22, MOR–CB1 = 20). Too-short (≤16) spacers bind univalently.
3. **Larger/looser interfaces need much longer spacers**: A2A–D2 = 43 atoms; D2/D3–NTS1 = 44–88 atoms; spacer length can even **discriminate D2 vs D3** heterodimers.
4. **Biphasic vs monophasic competition curves** and **heteromer-disrupting-peptide sensitivity** are the gold-standard tests distinguishing true bivalent bridging from enhanced-monovalent binding.
5. **Linker chemistry**: flexible PEG ("molecular universal joint") and alkyl chains dominate; rigid 1,2,3-triazole (CuAAC) and oligoproline scaffolds give defined geometry.
