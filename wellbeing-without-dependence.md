# Wellbeing Without Dependence: A Mechanistic Survey for Drug-Discovery Planning

*A literature-grounded survey of how analgesia and affective wellbeing might be decoupled
from tolerance, physical dependence, and withdrawal. Written for conceptual drug-discovery
planning — targets, mechanisms, and clinical evidence only. No synthesis chemistry.*

Date: 2026-06-11

---

## 0. Framing the question honestly

The original goal — pain relief without tolerance/dependence/withdrawal — is a tractable,
actively-pursued problem with at least one approved success (see §2). The reframed goal — a
"reliable, sustainable euphoriant" for the general population — is a categorically harder
problem, and it is worth stating plainly *why*, because the obstacle is mechanistic, not just
a missing molecule.

In the dominant neurobiological models of addiction, **euphoria is not incidental to dependence
— it is the input that drives it.** Two complementary theories make this precise:

- **Opponent-process / allostasis (Koob & Le Moal).** The brain answers a reliable hedonic
  "up" with a slow, recruited "anti-reward" process. With repetition the primary pleasure
  response *desensitizes* and the opponent process *sensitizes* — "reward allostasis" — so the
  reward set-point drifts downward. Tolerance and withdrawal dysphoria are the definitional
  consequences. The transition from recreational to compulsive use is precisely the point where
  the drug is taken to escape the opponent process rather than to feel the high.
  ([Koob & Le Moal, *Neuropsychopharmacology* 2001](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11120394/);
  [opponent-process review, *Phil Trans R Soc B* 2008](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18653439/))

A "reliable, sustainable euphoriant with no adaptation" is, in this framework, close to a
request to feed the reward system a strong signal indefinitely while forbidding it from
recalibrating. That is the engineering problem, and it is deep. **However**, there is one
serious scientific reason not to treat it as flatly impossible — the wanting/liking
dissociation (§1) — and that is where any honest search should actually begin.

---

## 1. The one real opening: "wanting" ≠ "liking"

The most important nuance for this whole project is **Berridge & Robinson's incentive-
sensitization theory**, now restated at its 30-year mark
([Robinson & Berridge, *Annu. Rev. Psychol.* 2025](https://sites.lsa.umich.edu/berridge-lab/wp-content/uploads/sites/743/2025/06/2025-Robinson-Berridge-The-incentive-sensitization-theory-of-addiction-30-years-on-An-Rev-Psychol.pdf);
[Berridge & Robinson, *Am. Psychol.* 2016](https://sites.lsa.umich.edu/berridge-lab/wp-content/uploads/sites/743/2019/10/2016-Berridge-Robinson-Liking-wanting-IS-theory-of-addiction-Am-Psychol.pdf)):

- **"Wanting"** (incentive salience) is mediated by robust, widely-distributed **mesolimbic
  dopamine** systems. It is what sensitizes — persistently, for long after drug use stops — and
  it is the motivational engine of compulsive use.
- **"Liking"** (actual hedonic pleasure) is generated in much smaller, fragile **opioid/
  cannabinoid "hedonic hotspots"** (e.g., nucleus accumbens shell, ventral pallidum).
- **They are dissociable.** Nicotine is intensely "wanted" while producing little euphoria.
  Dopamine drives wanting but does *not* itself produce liking.

The drug-discovery implication: addiction is primarily a disorder of **sensitized dopaminergic
wanting**, not of too much pleasure per se. In principle, a compound that engages **hedonic/
liking circuitry without driving dopaminergic incentive-sensitization** would be less
addictive than its subjective pleasantness suggests. This is the only mechanistically
respectable route toward the stated goal.

The catch, from the same literature: the hedonic hotspots are *small and downregulate* under
sustained stimulation ("liking tolerance"), while the dopaminergic wanting system is the part
that sensitizes. So even the optimistic reading does not deliver *unlimited sustained* euphoria
— it suggests pleasure can be made *less addictive*, not *non-adapting*. Durability and
non-dependence are partly in tension.

## 2. Pain: where decoupling is already proven (the template)

This is the success case that makes the analgesia version credible and provides the design
template — separate the therapeutic mechanism from the reward/dependence mechanism.

| Target / mechanism | Why it decouples | Evidence status | Key liabilities |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Naᵥ1.8 blockade** (suzetrigine) | Peripheral sensory-neuron sodium channel; blocks pain signal transmission without touching CNS reward circuitry | **FDA-approved 2025** for acute pain; non-opioid, non-scheduled | Efficacy in acute pain modest vs. opioids; chronic-pain breadth still being established |
| **Naᵥ1.7 blockade** | Loss-of-function humans are congenitally pain-free; peripheral, non-reward target | Repeated clinical disappointments (selectivity/PK); genetic validation strong, drugging hard | Hard to get isoform selectivity + CNS exclusion |
| **G-protein-biased μ-opioid agonism** (oliceridine) | Hypothesis: analgesia via G-protein arm, less β-arrestin → fewer adverse effects | Approved (oliceridine); **but** bias→safety story is now contested | Still a scheduled opioid with real dependence liability; "bias" benefit smaller than hoped |
| **Splice-variant μ agonists (e.g., IBNtxA / 6TM)** | Target alternative μ-receptor splice variants; preclinical analgesia with reduced reward/respiratory effects | Preclinical | Unproven in humans; selectivity uncertain |
| **Peripherally-restricted κ-opioid agonists** (difelikefalin) | κ agonism is analgesic/antipruritic; peripheral restriction avoids CNS dysphoria/sedation; κ is *not* rewarding (it's aversive) | **Approved** (difelikefalin, for pruritus) | Centrally, κ agonism causes dysphoria — the opposite of a euphoriant |
| **α2δ ligands** (gabapentin/pregabalin) | Modulate Ca²⁺ channel trafficking in sensitized neurons | Approved, widely used | Real misuse/dependence signal has emerged; not liability-free |
| **CB2 / peripheral cannabinoid** | CB2 is largely non-CNS, immune/inflammatory; avoids CB1 reward | Mostly preclinical | CB2-selective analgesia has under-delivered clinically |

**Takeaways for the pain goal:** the winning pattern is **peripheral restriction** or a
**non-reward molecular target** (Naᵥ channels, peripheral κ). The opioid-centric "biased
agonist" route has under-delivered on its dependence-free promise — a cautionary note for any
strategy that tries to keep the rewarding target and engineer away its downside.

Sources: Naᵥ1.8/suzetrigine and the broader non-opioid landscape are reviewed alongside
ketamine/neurosteroid work in
[*Rapid-acting modulators*, PMC 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12559931/);
opioid abuse-liability context in
[esketamine abuse-liability analysis, PMC 2025](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12179440/).

### 2.1 DFNZ (April 2026) — the strongest decoupling signal yet, and what it really shows
**N-desethyl-fluornitrazene (DFNZ)**, a nitazene-derived **µ-opioid superagonist**, produced
potent analgesia in rodents with — per the preclinical reports — **no clear tolerance, no NAc
µ-receptor downregulation, low withdrawal burden, and weak reward signaling**, even after a
month of escalating doses.
([C&EN, Apr 2026](https://cen.acs.org/pharmaceuticals/drug-discovery/new-opioid-painkiller-surprisingly-few/104/web/2026/04);
[MedicalXpress, Apr 2026](https://medicalxpress.com/news/2026-04-safer-opioid-dfnz-discovery-pain.html);
[Clin Lab Int, Apr 2026](https://clinlabint.com/nitazene-derivative-dfnz-delivers-potent-analgesia-without-respiratory-depression-or-addiction-signals-in-preclinical-models/))

**The mechanism is the lesson.** DFNZ's reported safety comes from *dopamine kinetics*: it
drives **slow tonic** dopamine release in the nucleus accumbens **without the fast phasic
dopamine transients** that encode reinforcement and cue-conditioned craving. In Berridge terms
(§1), it engages the receptor while **largely bypassing the phasic-dopamine "wanting" signal.**

This is the best modern evidence that analgesia and dependence can be pulled apart *even at the
µ-opioid receptor* — the hardest possible place to try. But read carefully what it implies for
the euphoriant goal:

- **DFNZ achieves safety by *subtracting* the reward/euphoria signal, not preserving it.** Its
  selling point is *weaker* dopamine-linked reward. It is, mechanistically, the **opposite of a
  euphoriant** — an opioid engineered to relieve pain while minimizing the high. It is powerful
  support for *analgesia without dependence*, and simultaneously evidence that **the euphoria is
  the liability you remove, not the feature you keep.**
- **It is preclinical and it is a nitazene.** Rodent "no tolerance / low reward" results have
  historically *not* survived translation (see §4 on the asymmetry of error). Nitazenes as a
  class are among the most potent and lethal street opioids; therapeutic index and human abuse
  liability are unestablished.

### 2.2 The set-point principle (the actual answer to "tolerance-free")
The reward literature gives a crisp boundary for *when* an effect can be tolerance-free
([Koob & Le Moal allostasis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11120394/);
[hedonic set-point overview](https://www.biopsychiatry.com/hedonicsp.htm)):
counter-adaptive opponent processes fire in response to **deviation above the homeostatic
set-point**. Therefore:

- **Correcting a deficit *toward* baseline** (lifting anhedonia/depression back to normal
  hedonic tone) does **not** require fighting homeostasis — the system is not being pushed into
  an allostatic state, so it need not be self-limiting. This is why KOR antagonists and
  single-dose psychedelics can give *durable* benefit.
- **Driving hedonic tone *above* baseline** (supranormal euphoria in a healthy person) is
  exactly the stimulus that recruits the downward recalibration. "Tolerance-free sustainable
  euphoria" in the well is the case the homeostatic machinery is built to prevent.

**The mechanistic source of sustainability is operating at-or-below the set-point (deficit
correction), not above it.** That is not a moral claim; it is where the biology stops fighting
back.

## 3. Affect / "wellbeing": the legitimate therapeutic frontier

For trauma, depression, and anxiety, the productive reframing is **treat the deficit (anhedonia,
negative affect) rather than induce euphoria in the healthy.** This is where real, recent
clinical progress lives — and notably, several of these mechanisms are *anti-reward-system*
rather than reward-agonist, which is exactly why they avoid dependence.

### 3.1 Kappa-opioid receptor (KOR) antagonism — the most on-point mechanism
KOR is the brain's *endogenous dysphoria/anti-reward* receptor. Blocking it is hypothesized to
**lift anhedonia by disinhibiting dopamine in reward pathways** — restoring normal hedonic
capacity rather than supplying an external high.

- **Navacaprant** (300-fold KOR-selective): Phase 2 in MDD **missed its primary HAMD-17
  endpoint** (P = 0.121, n=171), **but** significantly improved anhedonia (SHAPS) — −2.8 to
  −3.4 points vs placebo (P=0.004 / P=0.002), and −4.8 (P=0.001) in the moderate-to-severe
  subgroup. Critically for this project: **no opioid agonist activity, no opiate-withdrawal
  signs on COWS, fewer adverse events than placebo (35.3% vs 44.1%), no SAEs.**
  ([Navacaprant Phase 2, PMC 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12105956/))
- **Aticaprant** (30-fold KOR-selective): Phase 2 adjunctive in TRD; well tolerated.
  ([Aticaprant review, *Expert Opin. Emerg. Drugs* 2024](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/14728214.2024.2345645);
  [systematic review of KOR antagonists for depression, *J. Affect. Disord.* 2024](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0165032724010863))

**Why this matters most:** KOR antagonists are an *antagonist* class with **no intrinsic reward
signal and no dependence/withdrawal signature** — they raise hedonic capacity by removing a
brake, not by pressing an accelerator. That is the cleanest available embodiment of "improve
wellbeing without the addiction tax." The honest caveat: efficacy so far is modest and
anhedonia-specific, not a general euphoriant.

### 3.2 Psychedelics (psilocybin) — durability from a *single* dose
The standout property is **non-dependence with durable effect**: a single 25-mg dose produces
rapid, sometimes weeks-to-months-long antidepressant effects, with **low physical-dependence
potential** and rapid pharmacodynamic tolerance that *discourages* repeated use (5-HT2A
downregulation) — the opposite of a reinforcing euphoriant.
([Single-dose psilocybin RCT, *JAMA*-class, PMC 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10472268/);
[single- vs two-dose meta-analysis, PMC 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11352277/);
[severe-TRD open-label, *Am. J. Psychiatry* 2024](https://psychiatryonline.org/doi/10.1176/appi.ajp.20231063))
- **Liabilities/open questions:** durability beyond ~12 weeks unproven; effect depends heavily
  on psychological support/set-and-setting (not a standalone pill); acute psychological risk
  requires monitored dosing; psychological (not physical) dependence possible.
- **Design lesson:** durability decoupled from daily exposure is achievable — the therapeutic
  value comes from a *plasticity window*, not from chronic occupancy. This sidesteps the
  opponent-process trap by *not* asking for sustained receptor stimulation.

### 3.3 Rapid-acting NMDA & GABAₐ modulators — fast relief, with caveats
- **Ketamine / esketamine** (NMDA modulation): genuinely rapid antidepressant effect, FDA-
  approved (esketamine) with continuation-phase relapse-prevention support. **But this class
  has the clearest abuse-liability of the bunch** — dissociative euphoria, real-world ketamine
  misuse, plus cystitis with heavy chronic use. It is the cautionary example of "euphoria-
  adjacent mechanism → dependence risk."
- **Neurosteroids** (brexanolone, zuranolone; GABAₐ positive allosteric modulators): mechanistic
  novelty and approval (brexanolone for postpartum depression), but **zuranolone monotherapy
  effect size was small enough that FDA declined the MDD indication.** Chief liability is
  sedation; GABAₐ PAMs carry intrinsic dependence concerns by class analogy.
  ([Rapid-acting NMDA/GABA review, PMC 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12559931/);
  [emerging antidepressant mechanisms, PMC 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12785913/);
  [esketamine abuse-liability, PMC 2025](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12179440/);
  [esketamine long-term safety SUSTAIN-2](https://www.psychiatrist.com/jcp/long-term-safety-of-esketamine-nasal-spray/))

## 4. Which avenues are most promising — and the honest verdict

**Ranked by "wellbeing/relief per unit of dependence risk," strongest first:**

1. **Non-reward molecular targets for pain (Naᵥ1.8 → Naᵥ1.7).** Already proven decoupling
   (suzetrigine approved). The cleanest win because the target has nothing to do with reward.
2. **KOR antagonism for anhedonia.** An *antagonist* with no reward signal and no withdrawal
   signature — mechanistically the best fit for "raise wellbeing without addiction." Efficacy
   modest so far; needs the right population (moderate-to-severe, anhedonia-dominant).
3. **Single-dose / plasticity-window agents (psychedelics).** Durability without chronic
   exposure structurally dodges the opponent-process trap. Best evidence for *lasting* mood
   benefit; logistically heavy and not a self-administered pill.
4. **Peripherally-restricted agents (peripheral κ, CB2).** Good safety logic; clinical delivery
   has lagged the rationale.
5. **NMDA/GABAₐ rapid-acting agents.** Real and fast, but the highest dependence/abuse liability
   of the therapeutic options — useful, not the template.

**The verdict on "a reliable, sustainable euphoriant for everyone":** the neuroscience does not
support it as stated. The opponent-process/allostasis machinery guarantees that *sustained,
reliable* reward signaling is met by downward recalibration — tolerance and withdrawal are the
system working as designed. The single genuine loophole, Berridge's wanting/liking dissociation,
says you can make pleasure *less addictive* (by avoiding dopaminergic incentive-sensitization),
**not** that you can make it *non-adapting* — the hedonic hotspots themselves downregulate.

The strategically correct target is therefore **not euphoria but anhedonia and negative affect**:
*restore* a depressed person's capacity for ordinary pleasure (KOR antagonism), open a
*plasticity window* for durable change (psychedelics), or *remove the pain* at a non-reward
target (Naᵥ channels). Each of these delivers the humanitarian payload — relief for people with
chronic pain, trauma, depression, anxiety — precisely because it sidesteps, rather than fights,
the reward system's homeostatic accounting. That, not Soma, is where the search should go.
